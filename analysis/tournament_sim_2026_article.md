# I Played the Rest of the Season 5,000 Times. Nebraska Won the Title in Half of Them.

Selection Sunday is five weeks away. That is a long time to wait for an answer we mostly have already, so I didn't. I took the 1,326 matches left on the Division I schedule and played them out 5,000 times with the ratings model behind my app. Every run built a 64-team field, seeded it, and played the whole tournament. Then I counted.

Nebraska is a 1 seed in all 5,000. It is the overall No. 1 in 91% of them, and it wins the national title in 2,450.

I built every field two ways. One follows RPI, because that is what the committee leans on. The other follows my power ratings, which care who is good rather than who scheduled cleverly. Nebraska does not care which one you use. The Huskers are the top overall seed in 91% of the RPI brackets and 99.8% of the ratings brackets. They win the Big Ten outright 99.6% of the time, so the automatic bid is paperwork.

The path, in all 5,000 runs:

- Win twice in Lincoln and reach the regional semifinals: 98%
- Reach the regional final: 91%
- Make the Final Four: 81%
- Reach the title match: 67%
- Win it: 49%

The first-round opponent is most often Alabama A&M, the projected SWAC champion. Condolences. The likeliest national semifinal opponent is Kentucky. When Nebraska reaches the final, Pittsburgh is waiting 42% of the time.

One honest caveat. My model runs a couple of points hot on favorites this season, and that compounds over six rounds. Call it 40 to 49% and you will be closer to right. Even the low end is double anyone else's chance.

The rest of the title odds: Pittsburgh 19%, Louisville 10%, Kentucky 6%. Wisconsin, whom the Big Ten keeps insisting is our rival, makes the Final Four 24% of the time and wins it 2.5%.

The Big Ten sends about 11 teams by RPI and 12 by the ratings, and about five of them host first-round pods. The one fight worth having is Michigan. My ratings put the Wolverines in the field 96% of the time. RPI does it 36%. RPI sees a 13-7 record. The ratings see every rally, and the rallies say tournament team.

RPI has pets, too. Western Kentucky makes every RPI field and hosts in 76% of them. The ratings leave the Hilltoppers home more than half the time. LMU and DePaul ride RPI into the bracket at least two times in three, and almost never by the ratings. When the bracket drops, check those three. They will tell you which math the committee used.

Five thousand seasons, and the answer barely moved. Nebraska is a coin flip against the entire sport. I will take that flip every year.
